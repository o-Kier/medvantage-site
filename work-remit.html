Concept · Medical billing

Remit: a billing website that shows consultants where the money goes.

Consultants don’t hand over their billing because a website sounds confident. They hand it over when they can see exactly what they’ll get back. Remit shows them before the first call.

1

The brief

A billing company asks consultants to trust it with their income. The site has to make that feel safe and checkable.

We designed for a consultant or practice manager reading in their own time, sceptical by default. The site needed to show three things quickly: what changes for them (very little), what they pay (one number), and how they’ll know it’s working (a monthly statement).

2

What billing sites usually miss

Looking across UK medical billing websites, the same gaps come up again and again.

  • Pricing on request

    The first thing a consultant wants to know sits behind a sales call.

  • Promises without proof

    “Maximise your revenue”, with no picture of what the reporting actually looks like.

  • One page for every specialty

    Copy that could be for a dermatologist or a surgeon, so nobody feels understood.

  • Patient data as a footnote

    It’s the biggest worry about outsourcing billing, and it’s usually a line in the privacy policy.

3

Key decisions

Each section of the site answers one question a consultant has before they let someone else handle their billing.

01

A name that says the job

To remit is what an insurer does when it pays, so the name is short, plain and exactly about getting paid. The mark, two leaves and two coins turning about a centre, suggests money coming back round, and it makes a quarter turn when you hover over the logo.

02

Show the statement, not the claim

Instead of promising transparency, the site shows the monthly statement itself: invoiced, collected, days to payment and what’s still owed. It moves through three example months, so the numbers visibly improve as you watch.

03

Prove it knows the specialty

Rather than speaking to every consultant at once, the site picks gastroenterology and shows its working. An example endoscopy list is checked line by line, flagging a fee shortfall and a sedation question a generalist might miss.

04

One number for pricing

5% of what’s collected, set large enough to be the whole section, with the terms beside it as a plain list. Just above, a calculator lets visitors run their own figures first, and it says so honestly when Remit would cost them more.

05

A ledger, not a brochure

One confident blue marks money coming in, set against white and a cool stone grey, with amber kept for anything that needs attention. The type is chosen to read like a well-kept account book.

4

Just as clear on a phone

Consultants often read between lists. The headline stays big, the statement folds neatly into two columns, and the 5% still fills the screen.

5

What a real company would add

Remit is a concept, so some things are deliberately left out. For a real billing company, we’d build these in with the team:

  • Their ICO registration and security certifications, linked to the public records
  • Figures taken from real client statements, not illustrative ones
  • Named account managers, with photographs
  • A page for each specialty they bill for
  • Feedback from the consultants and practice managers they work with
